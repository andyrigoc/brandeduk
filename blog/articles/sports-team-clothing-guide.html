Sports Team Clothing and Clubwear Guide

Plan playing kit, staff clothing, travel wear and supporter garments as one coordinated range.
By Branded UK Production Team · 22 September 2026 · Reviewed by Anderson Ricotta
IMAGE REQUIRED
sports-team-clothing-clubwear-guide.webp

Plan sports team clothing for players, coaches, staff and supporters with suitable products, logo positions and sponsor branding.

Separate performance kit from club clothing

Playing garments, warm-up layers, travel wear, coach clothing and supporter merchandise serve different purposes. Sports team clothing works best when these groups are planned together but not forced into one fabric or fit.

Use shared colours, club marks and sponsor rules to keep the range connected.

Choose products by activity

Performance T shirts and polos can support training and coaching. Personalised hoodies, sweatshirts and jackets provide warm layers before and after activity. Custom T shirts can serve events, fundraising and supporters.

Check mobility, drying time, fit and layering. A product that works for travel may not perform during training.

Control crests names and numbers

Maintain one approved crest and sponsor artwork pack. Confirm positions, sizes and colours for each garment type. Names and numbers should be supplied on one final data sheet to reduce spelling and duplication errors.

Embroidery can suit crests on polos, caps, hoodies and embroidered jackets. Printing is normally more practical for large numbers, names and back graphics.

Plan size and quantity

Collect assigned player and staff sizes against the exact product guide. Merchandise stock requires a different approach based on previous sales, audience and event size. Do not apply the player size mix to supporter products without evidence.

Keep a small controlled reserve for new players and replacements where the club’s turnover justifies it.

Make reordering simple

Record every approved garment, colour and decoration setting. A club range should remain easy to reproduce during the season. Changing suppliers or near-matching colours without review can create visible inconsistency.
