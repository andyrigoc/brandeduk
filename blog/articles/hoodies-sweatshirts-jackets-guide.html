Personalised Hoodies Sweatshirts and Embroidered Jackets

Compare warm workwear layers and choose the correct logo method for each product.
By Branded UK Production Team · 22 September 2026 · Reviewed by Anderson Ricotta

Compare personalised hoodies, embroidered hoodies, custom sweatshirts and embroidered jackets for work, teams and events.

Choose the layer by working condition

Hoodies, sweatshirts and jackets solve different problems. Custom sweatshirts create a clean crew-neck layer for uniforms. Personalised hoodies add warmth and a more casual appearance. Jackets provide weather protection or insulation. Choose by environment and role rather than treating every warm garment as interchangeable.

A coordinated range can use all three while maintaining the same colours and chest logo.

Pullover and zipped hoodies

Pullover hoodies provide an uninterrupted front and work well for teams, trades and events. Zipped hoodies are easier to remove and can support changing indoor temperatures. Pockets, drawcords, zips and seams affect the available branding area.

Embroidered hoodies work well with compact logos. Large back artwork is normally better printed.

Crew-neck sweatshirts

Custom sweatshirts offer warmth without a hood and can look more uniform beneath jackets. Check cuff recovery, waistband, fabric weight, fit and wash care. Polycotton blends are common for everyday workwear, while premium cotton-rich styles can create a softer retail feel.

Chest embroidery is a dependable choice. DTF or screen printing can support larger graphics.

Embroidered jackets

Softshells, fleeces, padded jackets and waterproofs behave differently during embroidery. Lining, insulation, coatings, panels and access points must be assessed. Embroidered jackets should never be approved solely from a flat product image.

Some technical garments require a print method or a different branding position to protect performance and appearance.

Create a consistent warm layer range

Limit the approved colours and use one logo specification wherever the garment construction allows. Record any necessary position changes for pockets, panels or zips. Staff should be able to combine layers without the branding appearing inconsistent.

Order enough mid layers for the working pattern, but avoid excessive stock. Review actual use after the colder season before repeating the quantity.
