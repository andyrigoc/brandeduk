How to Plan Corporate Uniforms and Staff Uniforms

Create branded uniforms that look consistent, fit different roles and remain easy to reorder.
By Branded UK Production Team · 22 September 2026 · Reviewed by Anderson Ricotta
IMAGE REQUIRED
corporate-staff-uniforms-planning-guide.webp

Plan corporate uniforms and staff uniforms by role, garment, colour, logo placement, allocation and repeat ordering.

Define roles before choosing products

Corporate uniforms should reflect what employees actually do. Reception, office, warehouse, delivery and management teams may need different garments, but they can still share colours and branding. Start by grouping roles with similar working conditions rather than ordering one product for everyone.

This makes staff uniforms more comfortable and improves the likelihood that employees will wear them correctly.

Build a controlled product range

Choose a core top, warm layer and outer layer for each role. Keep the range tight enough to maintain consistency. Too many similar options make approvals, stock and repeat orders harder to manage.

Branded uniforms can include different fits without losing their identity. Use equivalent colours and logo placement across men’s, women’s and unisex garments where the range supports them.

Set colour and logo rules

Record approved garment colours, logo versions, thread or print colours, positions and sizes. If the company has light and dark logo variations, specify which one belongs on each garment colour.

A chest logo normally provides sufficient recognition for customer-facing roles. Larger back designs can support delivery, event or service teams where distance visibility matters.

Calculate employee allocations

The number of garments depends on working days, laundry frequency, role and season. Many full-time employees require several everyday tops but fewer mid layers and jackets. Part-time and occasional staff need a different allocation.

Keep a controlled reserve for common sizes and new starters. Large speculative stock ties up cash and can become unusable if the product or branding changes.

Create a repeat-order system

Store the approved product codes, colours, sizes, artwork and personalisation data in one place. Managers should reorder from this range instead of choosing new products each time. This protects brand consistency and shortens the approval process.

Review replacement rates and staff feedback after the first season. Real usage data is more useful than a fixed uniform rule borrowed from another company.
