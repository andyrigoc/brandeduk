Hospitality Uniforms for Cafés Restaurants and Hotels

Plan practical hospitality uniforms that stay comfortable through service and support the customer experience.
By Branded UK Production Team · 22 September 2026 · Reviewed by Anderson Ricotta
IMAGE REQUIRED
hospitality-uniforms-cafe-restaurant-hotel.webp

Choose hospitality uniforms for cafés, restaurants, bars and hotels by comparing roles, fabrics, fits, aprons and logo methods.

Plan by role and environment

Front of house, bar, kitchen, housekeeping, delivery and management teams work differently. Hospitality uniforms should recognise those differences while maintaining one visual identity. A shirt may suit reception, while personalised polo shirts or T shirts can work better in active casual service.

Outdoor areas and deliveries may also require a branded layer that is not used inside.

Choose fabrics for heat movement and washing

Hospitality garments experience heat, movement, spills and frequent laundering. Check breathability, drying time, opacity, stain visibility and care instructions. The fabric should support the shift rather than only look good in a photograph.

Test the actual colour. Black can look smart but may show flour and lint. Light colours can create a clean appearance but need an appropriate laundry and replacement plan.

Use aprons as part of the uniform

Bib, waist and cross-back aprons create a strong branding area and help distinguish roles. Compare pockets, strap adjustment, fabric weight and wash care. Position the logo where it remains visible when the apron is tied and the pockets are used.

Embroidery gives stable apron fabrics a premium finish. Printing can suit larger or more detailed artwork.

Offer suitable fits

One nominally unisex garment will not suit every team member. Where possible, offer equivalent fits while keeping colours and branding consistent. Use product-specific size guides and sample fitting for larger staff groups.

Comfort matters commercially. Staff who feel restricted or overheated are less likely to wear the uniform as intended.

Keep repeat orders controlled

Hospitality teams can change throughout the year. Record product codes, colours, logo settings and employee sizes so new starters receive the correct garments quickly. A controlled range prevents gradual changes that make the team look inconsistent.
