How to Choose Personalised Workwear for Your Team

A practical guide to custom workwear, branded workwear and company clothing with a logo.
By Branded UK Production Team · 22 September 2026 · Reviewed by Anderson Ricotta
IMAGE REQUIRED

Choose personalised workwear for your team by comparing garments, fabrics, logo methods, sizing and working conditions.

Start with the job rather than the garment

The best personalised workwear starts with the work being done. A garment that performs well at reception may be unsuitable for a warehouse, building site or commercial kitchen. Before selecting products, list where employees work, how much they move, whether they face customers and how often the clothing will be washed.

This approach prevents a common mistake: choosing one item because it looks good online and then trying to make it serve every role. Custom workwear should form a small coordinated system. Everyday tops, warm layers, outerwear and role-specific garments can share the same colours and logo positions without being identical.

Choose fabric by use and care

Cotton feels familiar and comfortable, making it a strong option for many T shirts and polo shirts. Polyester can dry quickly and suit active roles. Polycotton blends are widely used for branded workwear because they balance comfort, durability and easier care. Fabric weight matters, but it should never be treated as the only measure of quality.

Check the care label against the real laundry routine. If garments will be washed frequently, the product and decoration method must support that routine. A uniform that looks excellent on delivery but is difficult to maintain will cost more through early replacement.

Build a simple uniform range

Start with one main top, one mid layer and one outer layer where needed. A trade team may use printed workwear T shirts, embroidered workwear polos, hoodies and softshell jackets. A customer-facing team may prefer polo shirts, knitwear and smart jackets. Limiting the number of approved products makes repeat ordering easier and keeps the team consistent.

Use one value range, one balanced range and one premium range when comparing products. The cheapest option is not always the lowest-cost choice if it loses shape, feels uncomfortable or needs replacing sooner.

Match the logo method to the product

Embroidery gives company clothing with a logo a textured, professional finish and works particularly well on polos, sweatshirts, fleeces, caps and jackets. DTF and DTG can reproduce detailed colour artwork. Screen printing is strong for suitable designs at higher quantities. The correct method depends on the garment, artwork, position and order size.

Large areas should not automatically be embroidered. Dense stitching can feel heavy and may distort lighter fabrics. A compact chest logo can be embroidered while a large back design is printed, giving one uniform the right finish in both positions.

Approve one complete specification

Confirm the product code, colour, sizes, quantities, logo version, position, approximate size and customisation method before production. Check names, phone numbers and website addresses carefully. Save this approved specification so future orders reproduce the same branded workwear instead of starting again.
